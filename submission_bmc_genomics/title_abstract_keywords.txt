Single-cell foundation models represent genes in context, but reveal no context-specific gene function beyond co-expression
Background: Single-cell foundation models read a cell as a sequence of its genes and build, at every layer, a representation of each gene that depends on the other genes in the cell. Whether this contextual representation carries information about a gene's role in a given cell type, the single-cell counterpart of word sense in language models, has not been tested. Results: We measured contextual gene representations in MaxToki (two sizes), scGPT and STATE, in 12 Tabula Sapiens cell types and an independent bone-marrow dataset. A gene's representation changes with cell type in a gene-specific way: the same gene's change agrees across independent cells at cosine 0.76, against 0.001 for a gene-shuffled control. The effect is strongest in the first two layers, survives rank control (99%), replicates on the independent dataset (0.71), is partly learned (untrained model: 0.44 against 0.74 on the same cells) and does not grow consistently with model size. Genes that change more gain somewhat more from real context in next-gene prediction (partial correlation 0.16). The changes follow functional axes such as nuclear versus surface genes (z = 17 against random axes), but gene sets matched on size and co-expression give similar organisation (p = 0.08–0.38). Steering along the nuclear/surface axis, with large pushes, shifts the model's predictions for other genes (p = 0.0081); a second axis has no consistent effect and a third acts in reverse. Two tests for context-specific gene function, run in MaxToki-217M, were negative: transcription factors sit slightly closer to their curated targets than co-expression predicts, but not significantly more so where those targets are active. Functional organisation differs sharply between models (z = 8.1–18.4 for MaxToki, 3.2 for scGPT, 1.1 for STATE). Conclusions: Single-cell foundation models build real contextual gene representations. In MaxToki, training organises them along functional axes no more than co-expression does, and we found no context-specific gene function beyond co-expression. Reading a gene's changed role in a new cell type will likely need models trained on more than expression. The functional organisation of contextual change offers a simple metric for comparing architectures.
