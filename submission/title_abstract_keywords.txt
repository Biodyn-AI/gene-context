Single-cell foundation models represent genes in context, but reveal no context-specific gene function beyond co-expression
Single-cell foundation models build, at every layer, a representation of each gene that depends on the other genes in the cell. Whether this contextual representation shows how a gene's role differs between cell types has not been tested. We measured contextual gene representations in MaxToki (217M and 1B), scGPT and STATE on identical Tabula Sapiens cells from 12 cell types. Every model changes a gene's representation with cell type in a gene-specific way that reproduces across independent cells, but untrained models keep much of it, and a representation built from expression alone shows it too. In MaxToki-217M the change follows functional axes such as nuclear versus surface genes (rank-controlled functional-z +13.3 against random axes), and in all four models it follows co-expressed gene sets; none of three expression-only representations shows either. In no model is the functional organisation significantly stronger than that of gene sets matched on co-expression. Adding the nuclear/surface direction to half of a cell's genes shifts MaxToki's predictions for the other half more than random directions from the same genes; as the pushes exceed natural changes, this shows the model can read the direction, not that it uses it. In both MaxToki models, genes that change more gain slightly more from their own context in next-gene prediction. Two direct tests for context-specific gene function were negative in all four models, though they detect only clear effects. These models therefore build real contextual gene representations organised by co-expression, but we found no context-specific gene function beyond it.
