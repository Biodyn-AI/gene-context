Single-cell foundation models represent genes in context, but reveal no context-specific gene function beyond co-expression
Background: Single-cell foundation models build, at every transformer layer, a representation of each gene that depends on the other genes in the cell. Whether this contextual representation shows how a gene's role differs between cell types, the single-cell counterpart of word sense in language models, has not been tested. Results: We measured contextual gene representations in MaxToki (217M and 1B), scGPT and STATE on identical Tabula Sapiens cells from 12 cell types, and in MaxToki-217M also on independent bone-marrow data. In every model a gene's representation changes with cell type in a gene-specific way: the change agrees between two independent halves of the cells, with a cosine 0.74 to 0.87 higher than for a randomly paired gene. It is not explained by the gene's rank in the cell and replicates on the bone-marrow data. Untrained versions keep 36–88% of it, and a representation built from expression alone also shows it (+0.321). In MaxToki-217M the change follows functional axes such as nuclear versus surface genes (z = +13.3 against random axes; on this axis STATE shows less and scGPT little). In all four models it follows co-expressed gene sets. None of three expression-only representations shows either kind of organisation. In no model is the functional organisation significantly stronger than that of gene sets matched on co-expression (smallest p = 0.07), although this test detects only large differences. Adding the nuclear/surface direction to half of a cell's genes shifts MaxToki's predictions for the other half more than random directions from the same genes; as the pushes exceed natural changes, this shows the model can read the direction, not that it uses it. In both MaxToki models, genes that change more gain slightly more from their own context in next-gene prediction. Two direct tests for context-specific gene function were negative in all four models. Conclusions: Single-cell foundation models build real contextual gene representations, organised by co-expression. We found no context-specific gene function beyond co-expression, though our tests could detect only clear effects. Models trained on more than expression were not tested. Functional organisation separates some architectures but does not rank them reliably.
